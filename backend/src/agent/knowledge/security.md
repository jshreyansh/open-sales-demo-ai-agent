# Security & Compliance

Be honest here — this topic has one real, built thing and several things that are NOT yet real.
Don't let "compliance" in the product's own pitch (MLR-ready content) bleed into implying data-security
certifications that don't exist yet.

## What's real: MLR compliance workflow

The product has a genuine, configurable content-governance feature: approval stages and assignees
for the Medical/Legal/Regulatory (MLR) review process (Brand Review → Medical Review → Legal Review
→ Compliance Sign-off, with a full audit trail — this is what the MLR Review / Approvals page shows).
This is "compliance" in the sense of regulatory content review, not data security. It's fine to
describe this confidently and in detail — it's a core, shipped part of the product.

## What's NOT real yet: data-security certifications

There is no SOC 2, HIPAA, ISO 27001, or GDPR certification for this product today. "Data residency
options" and "compliance-mode content workflows" exist only as an Enterprise-tier roadmap bullet
point — not implemented, not certified, no supporting documentation.

**If asked about SOC 2, HIPAA, data residency, encryption, or any formal security certification:
say plainly that those aren't certified yet, note it's an active roadmap item for Enterprise
customers, and offer to connect them with the team for the current real answer.** Do not say
"HIPAA-compliant" or "SOC 2 certified" — that would be inventing a claim, which is exactly what the
instructions ask you never to do. This is a topic where getting caught overclaiming actually costs
a pharma prospect's trust, more than admitting a gap would.

## Compliance landscape for this industry (context, not a claim of what we have)

This is background so you can discuss the topic like someone who actually understands pharma
vendor security review — not a list of certifications the product holds. When a prospect asks
"are you SOC 2 / HIPAA / GDPR compliant," they're usually running a standard vendor-risk checklist;
it helps to know which of these actually apply to a marketing-content platform like this one, and
why, rather than treating every acronym as equally relevant.

- **SOC 2 (Type II)** — the single most commonly requested certification for any B2B SaaS vendor,
  pharma included. Attests to security/availability/confidentiality controls over time (Type II,
  not just a point-in-time Type I). This is the one to expect being asked for first, and the one
  most worth prioritizing on an actual roadmap.
- **ISO 27001** — the international equivalent/companion to SOC 2, more often specifically asked
  for by EU-headquartered or globally-operating pharma companies. Frequently requested alongside
  or instead of SOC 2 for multinational deals.
- **GDPR** — not a certification, a legal obligation. Applies if any EU-based HCPs, patients, or
  staff have their personal data processed — relevant the moment a customer operates in or serves
  the EU. Handled via data processing agreements (DPAs) and data-handling practices, not a badge to
  earn. Worth knowing India has its own equivalent, the DPDP Act (2023), relevant given the
  product's India-based pricing and customer base.
- **HIPAA** — a US law that applies to "covered entities" (providers, payers) and their "business
  associates." A marketing content platform isn't a covered entity itself, and most of what it
  processes (HCP prescribing/engagement data, campaign content) isn't PHI. It becomes relevant
  narrowly: patient testimonial videos and consent forms touch identifiable patient information,
  so an Enterprise customer needing a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) is a legitimate ask — not
  something the whole platform needs to be HIPAA-certified for.
- **SOC 1** — about controls over *financial* reporting, relevant mainly if a vendor's system feeds
  numbers into a customer's own financial statements. Occasionally asked for by large-enterprise
  procurement/finance teams as part of a standard vendor packet, but it's the least relevant of
  these five for a content-generation platform — don't over-index on it if asked.
- **21 CFR Part 11** — FDA rule on electronic records/signatures. Worth being aware of because the
  MLR approval audit trail (see above) is exactly the kind of thing this rule cares about, even
  though the product isn't formally validated against it today.

If a prospect asks which of these matter for their specific procurement process, the honest answer
is usually "SOC 2 first, ISO 27001 if you're EU/global, GDPR/DPDP as a legal handling practice not
a badge, HIPAA only narrowly for consent-form content, SOC 1 rarely" — and none of these are
certified today, so route back to the "not real yet" section above rather than claiming any of them.
